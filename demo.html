Before I show you the high-risk journey, I want to be clear that most governance should feel light. If a use is low risk, it takes the fast lane: ten yes-or-no questions on one page. If every answer is No, the Lead confirms it, it gets a light register entry, equality, human rights and privacy are checked by pointing to assessments that already exist, one person with the right delegation signs it off, and it gets checked once a year. Any Yes or Unsure, or any material change later, and it moves to the full route. So the effort follows the risk. Now let me show you the other end: a high-risk case.

The example

A damp and mould urgency tool

A new tool scores damp and mould reports and recommends an urgency band. A housing officer confirms every band.

Why it counts as high risk: it affects vulnerable residents, which is a mandatory escalation trigger, so the Lead and the AI Assurance Board are told straight away.

Here is the whole journey on one page, laid over the three checkpoints you already use: before deployment, after the pilot, and ongoing. Six steps. The service owner tells us once; triage works out the route; specialists only do the checks this case actually needs, at the same time rather than one after another; one decision paper goes to the forum that already holds the authority; the pilot proves the guardrails before go-live; and monitoring follows the risk tier. Underneath it all is one record, the AIR-ID, so nobody re-briefs the next board.

This is where the ease comes from. Each person does their own part once. The service owner doesn't have to know the governance map. Specialists don't re-ask what intake already captured. The Board gives one assurance opinion that every forum can use, and the decision stays with the forum that already holds the delegation.

After go-live

Watching it, and acting fast

Monthly performance review; quarterly formal review to the AI Assurance Board.

If something goes wrong

  1. The Service Owner pauses it straight away
  2. The Lead and the Board are told

Logged on the same record, so the history is never lost.

Measures shown are examples for this case; each system's monitoring plan sets its own.

Going live isn't the end. Because this case is high risk, it gets a monthly performance review and a quarterly formal review to the Board. For a tool like this I'd watch how often officers override the suggestion, whether it works equally well for different groups of residents, and complaints. And if something goes wrong, the service owner can pause it immediately without waiting for a meeting; the forum with the authority then decides what happens next.

Govern by exception, not by archaeology.

Every answer, check, decision and condition sits on one record, so nobody has to dig back through separate boards to find out what happened.

Proposed design, for Council confirmation

If I leave you with one thing, it's this. The aim is governance by exception, not by archaeology: the record is always there, so attention goes to the cases that need it, not to digging back through separate board papers to work out what was agreed.
